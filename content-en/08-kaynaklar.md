#### Akfen Holding SAP Project

Customer                      09 Oct 2017 · 4 min read

A project implemented simultaneously across Akfen Holding group companies delivered efficient and simplified processes.
With the SAP project that centralized the processes of all group companies operating in different sectors such as construction, energy, real estate and healthcare, and defined a common reporting structure, the Holding achieved lean and standardized processes and error-free, fast financial reporting. Meral Altınok, Budget, Reporting and Risk Management Coordinator at Akfen Holding, explained the details of the project.
Could you tell us about your company and its activities?
Founded in 1976, Akfen gained holding status in 1999. With around 420 employees, our organization invests in, manages and coordinates its subsidiaries and affiliates operating in industries such as airport management and operations, construction, seaport operations, maritime transportation, water distribution and wastewater services, energy and real estate.
Could you tell us about your project?
To centralize the common processes of all group companies operating in different sectors within Akfen Holding, such as construction, energy, real estate and healthcare, and to define a common reporting structure, we decided to proceed with standard solutions from the SAP S/4HANA portfolio. Because Akfen Holding and its group companies are publicly listed and report to the Capital Markets Board (SPK), Budgeting, Financial Consolidation and Disclosure Management applications were implemented simultaneously, in addition to the classic S/4HANA Digital Core. Through the SAP Fiori platform, a mobile solution map was also put in place to manage fast, anywhere decision-making and approval processes. The S/4HANA Cash Management application was also used for the first time with this project in a way that supports mobile processes. We completed the project in 1 year.
Why did you need the project?
By managing all group companies on a single integrated system, we wanted to minimize business processes that require manual intervention across the holding and eliminate human error. We aimed to support decision-making with more efficient, faster strategic reporting while maintaining data consistency and security. A flexible, parametric budget structure was also among our goals.
What benefits did you gain from the project?
The most important outcome of the project was achieving standards across the processes of all group companies under the Akfen Holding umbrella. We achieved full compliance in business and legal processes. We can now prepare financial statements and budgets much faster and without errors. With a central ERP system, we achieved data consistency and a stable financial system. We made approval processes across the holding easier and more traceable. We were also able to build scalable structures in our real estate and construction processes.
Why did you choose Çözümevi?
We reviewed all the processes of our group companies and identified the needs. In line with those needs, we decided to work with Çözümevi Danışmanlık, taking as a reference the implementations at similarly sized companies in the industry.
Future plans
And Gayrimenkul aims to implement a business intelligence solution in-house for online reports in the second phase.
What kinds of applications and innovations are you considering going forward?
To build Akfen Holding's Corporate Information Management infrastructure, we aim to roll out Document Management applications. In addition, we are reviewing the SuccessFactors solution to enable digitalization in our HR processes.

#
What Is E-Archive? What Is It For? Is It Legally Mandatory?

What Is E-Archive?

As stated in the Tax Procedure Law General Communiqué, E-archive covers sending an invoice created electronically to the recipient on paper or electronically, with the issuer retaining the second copy. The E-archive application is an e-transformation platform that allows invoices created electronically for taxpayers not registered in the E-invoice system and for non-taxpayers to be delivered, at the recipient's request, either electronically or as a printout of the invoice. Because the E-archive application works in integration with the E-invoice system, taxpayers who will use the E-archive application must be registered in the E-invoice system. The E-archive application can be adopted in two ways. Taxpayers who qualify to use the system can join it through their own IT systems or through a private integrator. For it to be clear that an invoice was created under E-archive, the invoice must carry the statement "Transmitted electronically under E-archive permission." For sales made over the internet, the statement "This sale was made over the internet." is mandatory on the E-archive invoice. In addition, if the E-archive invoice is issued at the time of delivery of goods, it can replace the waybill. For this, the statement "Serves as a waybill." must be written on the invoice. For E-invoice-registered taxpayers to use the E-archive application through their IT systems, they must complete the certifications required by the Revenue Administration (GİB). Taxpayers who complete these certifications apply to the Revenue Administration in line with the conditions described in the e-Archive Application Application Guide. E-invoice users who will use the application through a private integrator apply directly to the private integrator and do not apply separately to the Administration. Whichever method is used, where the taxpayers using the application store the E-archive invoices they create is their own choice. What Is Embedded Analytics?

Embedded Analytics is a set of analytical solutions that come within S/4HANA, developed with the multidimensional data modeling approach of ABAP CDS Views, through which data is presented. Just like the BI Content offered for SAP BW, it contains ready-made CDS View content, modeled by SAP in line with business processes and offered under the name VDM (Virtual Data Model). After it was announced that the HANA Live product would not be supported with S/4HANA, Embedded Analytics emerged as an integrated product on HANA with the CDS infrastructure. This is because the CDS infrastructure is positioned as a common development concept for ABAP, HANA XS, the production of OData and REST services, and SAP Analytics. HANA Live was transitional content and has completed its mission. However, Calculation Views will continue to be used, particularly for scenarios involving Smart Data Access (SDA) or non-S/4HANA data kept in a different schema. The Evolution of Real-Time Analytics on HANA

With the arrival of HANA, we started producing many real-time scenarios to benefit from this performance on the OLAP side as well, and we reached today through the following developments: - Building custom reporting models on data replicated from the source OLTP system onto a HANA Standalone database using tools such as SLT or Data Services - Using HANA Live Content directly on the live system for Business Suite on HANA scenarios - Producing hybrid models with BW on HANA - Embedded Analytics with S/4HANA

Physical SAP BW Cubes

When OLAP scenarios that require data replication, such as the SAP BW solution, need to be used, cube design is the first step needed for an accurate and high-performing reporting system. Here, data is taken from the OLTP system, which consists of normalized tables, denormalized and transferred to BW cubes. Dimension designs are made with fields that will produce minimal Cartesian records, and where needed, line items are used to relate the attribute table directly to the fact table, resulting in an ideal cube structure. On the Embedded Analytics side, the design is only at the metadata level. In other words, we do not deal with table modeling on the HANA Database and no physical data replication takes place. This stems from S/4HANA's OLAP and OLTP capabilities alike. This truly creates an impressive meaning in an IT world where OLAP and OLTP systems have until now been positioned separately. We will cover in detail, in other articles, SAP HANA's ability to behave as both an OLAP and an OLTP system within the same system.

Embedded Analytics Cubes

Multi-dimensional data models are produced by developing with metadata that provide analytical features. In terms of the SAP ABAP CDS infrastructure, when a view written using DDL and annotations is activated, runtime objects are generated. The generated objects are metadata that work together with a DDL view, which we can see in the ABAP Workbench, and with the HANA Calculation Engine and Analytic Engine. As the name VDM indicates, these are virtual data models. In their most basic form, they are database views that read directly from operational tables and have been given summarization and analytical capabilities.
Two-Dimensional and Multi-Dimensional Data Models

The first distinction to make here is to grasp the difference between CDS Views, in which tables are related using classic joins, and Embedded Analytics Views. Two-dimensional data is represented as table-level data structures consisting only of rows and columns. In multi-dimensional data models, data is first split into two categories: dimensions and metrics. Dimensions are the attributes of the data, while metrics are quantitative measures that can be aggregated and summarized, that is, numeric fields. Two-dimensional data requires writing a different select statement for each navigation step, whereas multi-dimensional data offers the analytical features specified in its metadata through BI OLAP Tools that generate MDX. This metadata is also processed on the HANA Analytics Engine and shown to the end user.
BI OLAP Tools

What BI OLAP tools aim for is to let analysts examine multi-dimensional data interactively from different perspectives.  For a reporting tool to be OLAP, it fundamentally needs to offer summarization, drill-down and drag-and-drop features*. Models or queries created with Embedded Analytics can be called and displayed through the HANA OLAP connection with analytical tools that support an OLAP connection, such as Analysis for Office, Analysis for OLAP, Design Studio, Fiori OAnalysis or Crystal Reports. In addition, Embedded Analytics models can be displayed in the Bex Query Tool as a Transient Infoprovider and used as a source info provider in query design. However, one of the most striking developments here is that analytical queries can also be run with Embedded Analytics without using Bex.* O'Brien & Marakas, 2011, p. 402-403

When starting reporting projects in S4/HANA, the situation we encounter most often is that, when classifying reports, it is not clearly decided whether a report will be analytical (OLAP Embedded Analytics) or in a flat tabular format (ABAP ALV). It will help here to understand the concepts of analytical and tabular.
Previously, reporting was mostly decided by platform, either BW or transactional systems such as ERP/CRM/ISU. When BW was chosen, since it is itself an analytical platform, queries were naturally produced as analytical queries and displayed through BW Analyzer or SAP Portal. On transactional systems such as ERP, ABAP ALV reports in flat tabular format were written.

Analytical Report

These are reports in which navigation such as drag-and-drop, drill-down and drill-up is possible, key and text fields and unit-of-measure fields are linked to one another, and, most importantly, metrics allow automatic summarization. The pivot table in Excel is also a display that works on this logic. For this reason, analytical reports are often called pivots in the industry as well. To give an example of the differences between analytical and flat tabular reports: in ABAP ALV reports, the key and text values of a field, such as material, are developed separately as two independent fields in each report's output. In analytical reports, a separate dimension is created for material, and the material key field and material text are related to each other through the dimension. The dimension modeling that is created needs to be built only once and can be used across all cubes and therefore query designs. Consider another scenario: a report output made up of year, month, day and amount fields. In an ABAP ALV report, when the day field is removed from the display, no summarization works on the amount fields. In other words, even though the day field is not visible on screen, rows are still repeated in the table as if the day field were there.  In this case, amounts at year and month level can be shown by using ALV's subtotal feature. This is because the ABAP code has produced a flat tabular output and cannot know that it should summarize when the level of detail in the report decreases. In analytical reports, a numeric field such as "amount" has a default aggregation property and summarizes automatically at whatever level of breakdown. This is managed by the Analytic OLAP Engine in HANA. This sets up a fully analytical modeling with features such as drag-and-drop, navigation and hierarchies.

In Embedded Analytics models, this property is assigned using the @DefaultAggregation: #SUM annotation. As a result, if there is a need to jump to a transaction code by clicking any value in the report, such as material, document number, customer or vendor, an ABAP ALV report is appropriate; if there is a need to navigate within the report to run different ad hoc analyses, use hierarchies or use quick pivot transformations, building analytical reports with Embedded Analytics models is appropriate.

Analytical Modeling Example
I would also like to talk about some technical details at the development stage. As you will recall from our previous article, we said that we add analytical capabilities by adding annotations to ABAP CDS Views. In this way, our adventure that started with CDS actually turns into a BI concept. Let's go over some important annotations. Annotations start with @ and can be view-wide or field-specific. Some annotations can be defined on both the view and the field, and give a property depending on where they are defined. View-wide annotations:

@Analytics.dataCategory: takes #DIMENSION, #CUBE or #FACT values and specifies the role of our model.

@Analytics.query: true says that this view is an analytical query developed on a #CUBE.

@VDM.viewType: takes the values #BASIC, #COMPOSITE or #CONSUMPTION depending on the nature of the data model.

@OData.publish: true publishes the model built as an OData service; it is required for analytical applications on the Fiori side. Field-specific annotations are used mostly when designing queries. Examples include:

@AnalyticsDetails.query.axis: with #FREE, #ROWS or #COLUMNS, specifies the axis on which fields will appear in the query.

@EndUserText.label: can specify the header texts of fields to be displayed in the end interface.

@DefaultAggregation: gives the desired numeric fields the metric (measure) property and assigns the summarization property.

CDS Table Function

With CDS views with parameters, introduced with ABAP 7.40 SP08, we became able to design CDS views with parameters at the development stage. Not long after, with ABAP 7.50, AMDP classes were adapted to return data in table type and CDS Table Functions emerged. As a result, in situations that would require writing complex SQL statements, AMDP methods containing all native SQL code, including window functions, written in ABAP AMDP classes can now be called from the CDS environment by being referenced with a CDS table function. With Fiori powered by services that talk to S/4HANA, the analytical OAnalysis reporting tool comes into view.

OData

OData is a universal REST data protocol. Because we now live in a world where we communicate with countless different systems, differing communication rules for each system make development harder.

In line with the need this creates, a standard was created with OData and a generally applicable communication protocol was provided.

The role of OData technology on the Embedded Analytics side is that the CDS Views created can be exposed as an OData service by adding a single piece of metadata. In this way, we can build the infrastructure to serve multi-dimensional data models via OData to SAP Fiori Applications, other Native HANA XS Applications or 3rd party applications, and we can show the results of analytical queries in dynamic live tiles on SAP Fiori Tiles. The most common applications using this modeling are the Master-Detail applications on SAP Fiori. By serving Embedded Analytics output via OData, client applications can navigate hierarchically over back-end data with just a single REST service. Embedded Analytics also includes standard transactional and analytical applications coming from a single repository on the Fiori Launchpad, with standard content positioned in SAP Business Roles (PFCG). The relevant PFCG roles contain Fiori Catalogs and Groups that can be used on the SAP Fiori side. Inflation rates that have reached a 40-year high in many countries are putting businesses in a difficult position. Cash flow problems and normalized price increases do not only affect operations. In this article, we will look at the concept of inflation accounting that prevents this situation, the legal regulation, and how inflation accounting is applied in SAP.
What is inflation accounting?
Inflation accounting is a special technique used to take into account the effect of rising or falling product costs on the financial results companies report. In periods when inflation is felt, inflation accounting becomes a necessity for painting a clearer and more realistic picture of companies' financial performance, especially in times like the one we are living through today. At this point, the figures in financial statements need to be recalculated to reflect current values during a period of high inflation. This is where inflation accounting comes into play.
Is inflation accounting mandatory?
Inflation accounting, which comprises a set of accounting models designed to correct the problems caused by historical cost accounting in a high-inflation environment, must be carried out in line with the principles set by the International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), the Turkish Financial Reporting Standards (TFRS) and the Tax Procedure Law (VUK).
With the Tax Procedure Law (VUK) amendment, published in the Official Gazette and put into effect on December 31, 2023, inflation accounting became mandatory in Türkiye. Taxpayers are required to prepare financial statements and make inflation adjustments as of the end of the provisional tax periods.
According to the final Communiqué published in the Official Gazette, taxpayers will adjust their financial statements dated 12/31/2023 (or, for those assigned a special accounting period, the end of the special accounting period ending in 2024) pursuant to provisional Article 33 of Law No. 213. Adjustments to financial statements for subsequent periods will be made depending on the conditions set out in paragraph (A) of repeated Article 298 of the same Law.
Who Can Apply Inflation Accounting?

- Large and Medium-Sized Businesses: Inflation accounting is particularly necessary for large and medium-sized businesses, because their financial statements are important decision-making tools for investors and other stakeholders.

- Publicly Listed Companies: Publicly listed companies should use inflation accounting to provide their shareholders and potential investors with accurate and reliable financial information.

- Businesses That Export and Import: These businesses, which are directly affected by exchange rate fluctuations, can use inflation accounting to express their costs and revenues more accurately.

- Financial Institutions and Banks: They can apply inflation accounting to provide inflation-adjusted data for financial reporting and credit assessment processes.

Which Assets Can Inflation Accounting Be Applied To?

- Fixed Assets: The valuation of long-term assets such as land, buildings and machinery must be adjusted to reflect the effect of inflation.

- Inventories: Inventory valuations can be subject to inflation accounting in order to accurately reflect the effect of inflation on costs.

- Receivables and Payables: Non-monetary receivables and payables must be revalued, taking the effects of inflation into account.

- Equity Items: Equity items such as capital and reserves can be reported at inflation-adjusted values rather than historical costs.

The concept of KVKK (Turkish Personal Data Protection Law), which entered our lives only recently, comes up at every turn. You can no longer get a person's contact information from someone else, because the legal obligations around unauthorized information transfer are considerable. At this point, both individuals and companies have started taking their own precautions. At Detaysoft, we use SuccessFactors functions to help our customers manage their KVKK processes. First, let us briefly explain what KVKK (the Personal Data Protection Law) is. KVKK is the set of rules established to protect people's fundamental rights and freedoms, above all the privacy of private life. It applies to both natural and legal persons. Explicit consent must be obtained from individuals to process personal data, which means the consent obtained must be provable.

We can explain the impact of KVKK on HR (human resources) processes with a few examples: Protection of personal data in recruitment processes: During the application process, organizations must base the collection, storage and processing of the data they obtain from candidates on defined principles. This data must not be shared with other organizations or persons for purposes other than the original one without the candidate's approval. Managing employee information under KVKK: Employees must be informed that records about them are being kept and what they will be used for. Employees should be given the opportunity to periodically check the records about themselves and correct incorrect information if necessary; an employee's request for information must be answered within the period specified in the law, and if it is not answered, the reason for refusal must be communicated to the person. When sharing information with an employee, if other employees' information is also related to the requesting employee's case (such examples may come up in documentation processes such as minutes), the relevant person's information must be hidden or the relevant person's approval must be obtained. Employees' consent is required to share their information with other organizations or persons. In addition, all information must be archived after certain periods.

Why SAP HANA and S/4HANA?

SAP HANA and S4HANA

SAP, starting with the R2 version in 1979 and moving to the R3 version in 1992, formed the foundation of the products in use today. In 2004 it renamed this foundation ERP, adding mainly web applications. From 1979 to today, the database architecture has been an RDMS (Relational Database Management System) structure, with new tables continuously added on top and optimized to work on big data. Today, the greatest need is performance, and understanding big data very quickly and simply has become a requirement of the business world. Keeping a single record, optimized in a way that could hardly be called a good fit for RDMS logic, in 14-15 tables including summary tables was a difficult, even impossible path for an ERP. Moreover, SAP's promise, one of its biggest claims, that it runs on every database and operating system had started to become a problem both commercially and technically. Remaining tied to the database solutions of Oracle and Microsoft, which produce ERP solutions like itself, could not be the right roadmap.

Considering the technical and commercial failure of SapDB/MaxDB, which SAP had earlier acquired and developed, there was no margin for error. It first acquired Sybase and obtained an RDMS database architecture on Sybase ASE; then the real big change came in 2011. Using cheaper hardware and memory technology, it developed In-Memory Database technology, that is, the HANA Database architecture. On HANA, reading became hundreds of times faster. This moved reporting products like BW onto the HANA platform. Now there was a powerful platform like HANA for reporting on and processing big data. In 2013, the ERP solution also gained HANA Database support, but there was a question mark here. The table architectures and application structure had been built around offloading the workload from the database. This prevented the advantages brought by HANA technology from being reflected in user performance. S/4 HANA, announced in 2015, is technically the version after R3. By using the Simple Database architecture it is named after (getting rid of summary tables), it takes up less space and is therefore a much faster structure. As the front end, Fiori interfaces running with UI5 support made interfaces built on 25-year-old foundations accessible from anywhere and very easily. In addition, real-time reporting was achieved with HANA Live. Why would we still need reports prepared from data as of one day earlier from BW systems, or better yet, why would we need a BW system at all? The answer to the question "Why S/4 HANA?" lies here. After 2025, SAP will run only on the HANA platform, and all development currently under way is concentrated on S/4HANA. The answer to the question "Why SAP HANA and S/4HANA?" is actually very simple. The world has changed and evolved. If they sold a 1979 model Şahin as a brand-new 2015 model, would you buy it?

Increasing competition in the business world, economic parameters and other factors have taken budgeting and long-term planning to a more important point than ever for companies. The budget processes of companies operating in many sectors such as manufacturing, services and finance can be designed and planned with relatively more basic assumptions. However, for companies that work on a project basis and have a long-term business model, budgeting and simulation processes are much more complex. In project-based budget work, contract and specification information and the effects of this information on budget periods can be budgeted only through very detailed and meticulous work. As the Detaysoft Budgeting and Analytics team, we have successfully moved the budget processes of many project-based companies to the SAP BPC platform. The biggest factor in the success of these projects was the right design and the implementation of flexibly designed budget structures. When preparing a project budget, the following 4 important processes must be designed and integrated correctly:
•    Budget integration of actual data

•    A calculation and planning infrastructure compliant with government regulations, covering both statutory and IAS standards

•    Planning of projects likely to materialize in the budget periods

•    Correct design of project closing transactions in the budget system

Actual records must be transferred to the SAP BPC system in full (for detailed information about SAP BPC, you can read the "What Is SAP BPC?" article). Data such as delivery invoices not yet converted to invoices, purchase requisitions and orders, inventory and WIP inventory, actual advances, and income and cost accumulated up to the budget period must be transferred to BPC. This data affects both new data planning and calculations. Another important detail in the project budget is summarizing data during transfers for the sake of system performance. While summarizing, the design must also be detailed enough to obtain healthy outputs from the system. The regulations for the VUK and IFRS ledgers show significant differences in the project budget. In particular, the cost of goods sold and revenue calculations of projects under IAS 11 involve significant changes. To calculate the IAS 11 cost correctly, the cost process must be run at the project and material level, taking the inventory effect into account. When making cost calculations, stock movements and transfers between projects and production sites must also be carried out in line with IAS 11 regulations. IAS 11 regulations change the balances in advance accounts, current accounts and inventory accounts. Entries created independently of one another in the VUK and IAS ledgers until project closing are equalized in the project closing period or warranty start period. To perform closing transactions correctly, all amounts created for both ledgers over the life of the project must be compared and reconciled. Contract information for probable projects in the budget period must be designed in BPC. This contract information must also be included in all calculations made for current projects. As long as the data generated during project budget work is created correctly, it can serve as base data for the project modules in the ERP. For this reason, while transferring data from modules such as SAP PS, Oracle Project Planning and MS Project and processing it in BPC, we also load the budget data into the ERP in a suitable format, enabling more accurate project plans. What's New in SAP Analytics – Part 1

Along with HANA, significant developments are taking place in analytics applications in the SAP world. Real-time reporting has become indispensable for customers using SAP Business Suite on HANA or S/4HANA. A customer who has invested in in-memory technologies no longer wants to run analyses on data from one day earlier, copy the same data one-to-one into a different environment and increase hardware costs, or struggle with ETL processes, and rightly wants to see reports in real time.

So how is real-time reporting done? First, we can talk about 2 types of reporting: operational and strategic.
Operational Reporting

These are reports in which analysis can be done on more detailed data obtained from transaction codes in SAP such as FBL3N and MB5B. Before HANA, when user needs in operational reports could not be met by standard reports, ABAP consultants wrote custom "Z" reports, or very detailed data was pulled into a data warehouse such as BW and calculations were done as much as possible in transformation rules or in fields added to the data source. Because calculations were mostly done while extracting data, performance was better than an ABAP report. With HANA, SAP BI consultants who know SQL can now also design a significant portion of operational reports using SAP BusinessObjects BI tools, with views they develop in HANA Studio. In this type of reporting, the concepts of calculation views and CDS views come to the fore. Briefly;

Calculation view: These are views in which transactional data can be used. They are developed at the database layer with the HANA Modeler perspective in HANA Studio, and the consultant does the development with a database user. They are mostly used for reporting with any BI tool. It is similar to developing a universe with the SAP BusinessObjects Information Design Tool. So that end users can build their own reports, field definitions can be made in a language they understand (for example, using MATERIAL instead of MATNR). Calculations such as currency conversion and unit-of-measure conversion can be done without writing code. For example, a calculation view can be created by joining the VBAP and VBAK tables with attribute views (or with master data tables such as MARA). Unlike other views, it can be created both graphically without writing code (like creating an InfoSet or composite provider in SAP BW) and as a script by writing SQL. By writing code here, all SQL functions can be used. However, the schema to be used must always be specified beforehand for the tables. CDS views are by default located in the SAPABAP1 schema, and calculation views by default in the _SYS_BIC schema. If you create calculation views graphically, then when you want to add a table later, you have to recreate the fields you changed as output. This is a significant problem of calculation views.

Figure – 1: Calculation view graphical design screen

Figure – 2: Calculation view script design screen
With HANA 2.0, the attribute views and analytical views that were previously used separately from calculation views are now created within calculation views. Briefly on these views as well: an attribute view is a view in which master data and texts can be used. Attribute views can be built from the MARA, MAKT, T134T and T023T tables and used throughout the project in calculation views or in universes. Because it is master data, aggregation, union and so on cannot be done; only joins can. It can be thought of like master data data sources in SAP BW. An analytical view is transactional tables associated with dimensions (tables or attribute views). It is similar to the star schema logic in a data warehouse.

CDS (Core Data Services) view: These are views developed on the ABAP layer with the ABAP perspective in HANA Studio. You can reach these views in SAP GUI with transaction code SE11.

Figure – 3: CDS view example
CDS views can be used in reports through a universe using SAP BusinessObjects BI tools, or through a calculation view. If Embedded BW is used, you can also connect directly and report with BEx Query Analyzer.

Because the views mentioned above run at the database level, calculations should be done in these views as much as possible so that reports perform well.

In operational reporting, the integration of views with SAP BusinessObjects BI tools gains importance. As can be seen in the screen below, there is a lot of flexibility in the integration. SAP tables can be used in CDS views. These CDS views can then be;

–    Used directly in BEx queries (only if Embedded BW is in place). If desired, they can be reported on from there with SAP BusinessObjects BI tools.-    Used within calculation views (and therefore in every environment where calculation views can be used). If desired, they can be reported on with SAP BusinessObjects BI tools without needing to create a universe.-    Used in universes with the Information Design Tool and reported on with SAP BusinessObjects BI tools.Data can also be used directly within calculation views, with or without CDS views. Calculation views, in turn, can alternatively be;-    Connected directly with SAP BusinessObjects BI tools and reported on without needing a universe.-    Used in universes with the Information Design Tool and reported on with SAP BusinessObjects BI tools.-    Used directly in BEx queries.

Figure – 4: SAP BusinessObjects BI integration
In addition to all this, SAP offers concepts it calls HANA Live and Embedded Analytics for operational reporting. HANA Live is a ready-made set of calculation views built using standard tables. To shorten report preparation and testing time in projects, SAP gathered the views it built from standard tables (for example, the SalesDocumentItem view from VBAP, VBUP and VEDA) under a content set called HANA Live. It also called this model the VDM (virtual data model). It can be thought of like the ready-made content data sources in SAP BW. In S/4HANA, SAP modified and developed this content to produce a structure called Embedded Analytics. Embedded Analytics is a ready-made structure consisting of CDS views instead of calculation views, with some ready-made reports from Fiori and SAP BusinessObjects BI applications on top of it.

What Is the SAP CO Module?

What Is the SAP CO Module?

The SAP CO module (Controlling module) provides an information flow to people at the relevant levels for tracking and reporting on internal income and expenses. It ensures that records created by other modules are generated in line with companies' reporting/analysis needs, and makes detailed cost and profitability analyses possible using this collected data. Because cost and profitability analyses are of vital importance to all companies, the SAP Controlling (CO) module is a module needed by all industries. The Controlling module can be grouped under 4 headings: cost center accounting and internal orders, product costing (planned costing and actual costing - material ledger), profit center accounting, and profitability analysis.
1. Cost Center Accounting and Internal Orders

It aims to record data coming especially from the Financial Accounting (FI) and Materials Management/Procurement (MM) modules in the relevant areas of responsibility (cost centers), in line with companies' organizational structures. Expense records collected in cost centers feed the product costing and profitability analysis modules in period-end (month-end) closing transactions. Internal orders, on the other hand, allow expense tracking at a lower level than cost centers, in details such as vehicle license plates, mobile phones, trade fairs and so on. In addition, it is possible to load expense budgets by cost center and internal order into the system and obtain plan/actual comparison reports.

2. Product Costing

It enables detailed tracking and analysis, by product, of expenses incurred during production such as direct raw material/material consumption, labor costs and depreciation expenses. With the material ledger function, actual costs can be calculated using the expenses incurred in each period (month) at breakdowns such as product/batch/customer order stock. In addition, calculating IFRS actual costs correctly is possible with the Alternative Valuation Run (AVR), a second material ledger. With this function, actual IFRS values are calculated by taking into account entries that cause different values in the VUK and IFRS ledgers, such as severance provisions, depreciation expenses and discounting of purchase prepayments, and the relevant accounting entries are recorded automatically in the IFRS ledger.
3. Profitability Analysis

Using sales data coming especially from the Sales & Distribution (SD) module and actual costs calculated at month-end, it enables detailed profitability analyses at breakdowns such as product/customer/customer group/country/sales group. Selling, marketing, general administrative and financing expenses accumulated in cost centers during the period can be allocated with allocation keys such as sales quantity/net sales revenue/gross sales revenue, and reported at breakdowns such as product/customer/customer group/country/sales group.
4. Profit Center Accounting

Profit centers can be created in parallel with the cost center structure or in a hierarchical structure at breakdowns such as department/region/product group. By assigning them within master data such as materials, cost centers, production orders and customer orders, it allows a balance sheet and income statement to be obtained at different department/region/product group breakdowns under the same legal entity. With transfer pricing, internal revenue/internal cost entries are created during stock transfers between departments, so that departments that do not normally make sales can also earn revenue.

HANA Developments in SAP ERP Systems, HANA and ABAP

HANA Developments in SAP ERP Systems, HANA and ABAP

Introduced in 2011, HANA has changed rapidly since the day it went into use and, together with S4HANA, now sits at the center of ERP systems. At Detaysoft, we followed every development closely during this process and analyzed them in our test systems to work out how to use them to improve our customers' processes. In this article, we will look at what has changed in ERP development logic with HANA, keeping HANA's historical evolution in mind as we do so.
The Early Days of HANA

Just two years ago, it was not possible to use HANA as the primary database in SAP ERP solutions. It was present in the system only as a secondary database; the tables needed for specific reports could be copied to the HANA database, and native SQL could be written and used where performance was required. At this stage, it was not possible to reach HANA tables directly with ABAP code. The only way to communicate directly with HANA from ABAP was through the exec sql command and ADBC, but this was neither practical nor a method anyone would choose for day-to-day work.

Code Pushdown

Before HANA, the database was the most critical bottleneck in ABAP development. Because connecting to the database consumed a great deal of time, we tried to retrieve as much data as possible in a single pass and did the calculations and processing at the application level. With HANA, the database is no longer a bottleneck and has even become the fastest part of the runtime. That is why, in post-HANA development, we try to perform most operations on the database wherever possible and pass only the result to the application level. This new approach, in which program logic is pushed down to the database, is called code pushdown.
Native Views, External Views and the ABAP Procedure Proxy Method

With ERP on HANA, many standard reports were converted using Analytical, Attribute and Calculation views, which let us benefit from HANA's performance. To make these usable in ABAP, the External View and ABAP Procedure Proxy methods were developed. With these methods, a view or procedure created in the database can be called from ABAP. An external view allows a view that lives in the database to be used in ABAP, while an ABAP procedure proxy uses an RFC-like connection to let database procedures be executed in a way similar to function modules. However, for two reasons, these methods are not practical or usable enough to let us fully exploit HANA's potential in everyday development. The first is that a significant part of the development has to be done on the database using native SQL; the second is that many programmers would have to be given the authority to develop directly on the database. In addition, building the database and application sides of a development independently of each other can cause many problems when the application goes live. For these reasons, outside of standard developments, the use of External Views and ABAP Procedure Proxy never became widespread and stayed at low levels.
OPEN SQL Changes After HANA: CDS Views and AMDP

The methods described above were a confusing situation for many ABAP specialists in the past. Would every ABAP specialist have to learn native SQL? How would the practical difficulties of using two different development platforms be overcome? Fortunately, the innovations and new tools that arrived in 2014-2015, first with ERP on HANA and then with S/4HANA, put these question marks behind us and gave us clear information about the future of ABAP.
CDS (Core Data Services)

Core Data Services is a powerful view-building tool adapted to ABAP. Another feature of CDS views is that, as with ABAP tables, they have a counterpart at both the application and the database level. A view you create can be used in select statements in ABAP, and another database can also access CDS views over an ODBC connection. With a syntax that is easy for anyone who has done ABAP development before, CDS views are a far more advantageous tool than the options offered earlier for applying the code pushdown principle in a practical way across many different jobs. As a secondary use, CDS views also let us design a large data source that contains all the data needed for similar kinds of information, so that different applications can use this single data source.
AMDP (ABAP Managed Database Procedures)

In situations where the tools at hand are not enough for HANA and the code pushdown principle, or in certain special scenarios, we may need to write native SQL code. The two tools we previously had for this were EXEC SQL and ADBC (ABAP Database Connectivity), but both had drawbacks such as not being able to see syntax errors at development time and using a layered structure. With AMDP, which like CDS can only be developed through Eclipse, we can write and check native SQL code just as we would in any database console. To use any ABAP method as an AMDP, all we have to do is add the IF_AMDP_MARKER_HDB interface to the interfaces of the method's class; after that, we can write native SQL code inside the method and use the method in ABAP developments.   OPEN SQL (ABAP Software Component Level 740)OPEN SQL consists of simpler statements that are easier to learn than native SQL. Until now, however, because of many limitations, it could not be called a very flexible tool for meeting needs such as calculation, filtering and grouping, and where it was not flexible, requirements were met with developments at the application level. The limitations that existed when tables were combined with joins required different tables to be accessed through separate selects. This led to a practice that was the exact opposite of the code pushdown principle and meant the performance potential of the HANA database was not used sufficiently. Thanks to the revolutionary new features added with ABAP software component 740 and later 750, and the removal of some existing limitations, we can now write larger and more functional select statements in ABAP that comply with the code pushdown principle, and we can achieve HANA's performance advantage by using the new features without depending on any other tool.
The Future of HANA ERP Development

From the changes we have seen so far and from the recently released ABAP 7.5, we can say that CDS, AMDP and OPEN SQL will keep gaining new features in upcoming releases and continue to play an important role in developments that run on the HANA database on the customer side, and that OPEN SQL, with the new features and functions it has gained, will move closer to native SQL.

What Is SAP GRC?

In this article, I will cover the SAP GRC solution in broad terms. Let's start with the answer to the question "What Is SAP GRC?": GRC, short for Governance, Risk and Compliance, is a solution that complements and unifies, as one interconnected process, the risk management of many processes such as integrated application systems, activities within the company and staged corporate management. SAP GRC brings added ease of management in resolving and auditing problems arising from fragmented company processes and, at the same time, from inconsistent strategies. The SAP NetWeaver application platform forms the technological basis for using SAP GRC. GRC systems built on independent operating systems are a prerequisite for continuous auditing and for the control and auditing of automated processes. The system is designed for different areas such as corporate governance and the auditing of these provisions, risk management, access and authorization distribution, as well as the management of the conditions and regulations that apply in international trade.

SAP GRC Access Control manages access and authorization distribution within the company in line with the prescribed rules. For example, a user who opens a sales request should not approve the sale, and a user who makes a stock entry should not perform the stock count. Access Control makes it easier to manage and audit the dangers and risks arising from authorization distribution.

Risk Analysis & Risk Remediation

Risk identification arising from authorizations and roles within the company, and risk remediation based on SAP roles, are carried out through GRC. Based on a pre-built rule catalog, GRC reveals the risks in authorizations and contributes to the remediation process. With SAP GRC you can produce different risk analysis reports:

•    Action Level: Can be used to perform SoD (Segregation of Duties) analysis at the transaction code level.

•    Permission Level: Used to perform SoD analysis at the authorization object and activity level.

•    Critical Actions: Can be used to analyze users who have access to a critical function.

•    Critical Roles/Profiles: Can be used to analyze users who have access to critical roles or profiles.
Enterprise Role Management

SAP systems manage authorizations with authorization roles prepared according to company requirements. Access Control puts role management and role building in the hands of the company departments that use the roles directly. Role managers who adapt the roles needed for their own authorizations create compliant, risk-free roles by using the rule and risk catalog pre-built in GRC.

Compliant User Provisioning

Users who need access to the SAP system go through a pre-established approval process. Within this approval process, the approvers run the authorizations requested by the new user through the risk catalog filter once more, ensuring that access is secure within the SAP system.

Super User Privilege Management

The management of temporary additional authorizations granted in SAP systems usually creates certain gaps in company security. So that users can obtain additional authorizations in certain situations, Access Control takes on the role of a firefighter (Fire Fighter) and provides a short-term expansion of authorization. During this access, which is provided in line with the company's security rules, everything that is done is reported in a detailed audit log.In summary, with SAP GRC Access Control you can speed up the process by which users access applications and data. With SAP GRC Access Control you can automate access provisioning and verification and use preventive policy controls. In addition, you can monitor emergency access activity and let business owners manage system access with minimal IT support.In this article, we covered the SAP GRC solution in broad terms. SAP IBP – Process Template

Process templates contain a group of processes created for building and finalizing a demand or supply plan. Process management helps you define, manage and monitor planning processes. It also offers automation features to help reduce manual tasks and support collaboration among the people involved in the process. Each of the events in the process represents a stage in the process and has its own individual duration. The people participating in each stage come from different functional groups. In addition, there is a different set of activities to be carried out at each stage. There are different requirements that must be met before a process stage can be completed and the next stage started.

Planning processes are usually repeated in recurring cycles. Since you would have to set up a new process from scratch every time you want to repeat the planning cycle, you can create a template beforehand. In this template, you can define your company's planning process and all its important aspects. First, you model the individual process stages, represented by process steps, that drive the structure of the process.

For each of these process steps, you can define its duration, the participants involved and the tasks they need to perform. Optionally, you can also configure step dependencies and automation settings for your process, and, if your system is connected to SAP Jam, you can add an SAP Jam group for collaboration. Once you have created your template, you can reuse it for each recurring period and create a new process from it.

Owners: The process step owner is responsible for starting, finishing and marking the process step as completed.
Participant group: In process management, the participant group is a user group that you define in the user groups application. Organizing participants into groups makes it easier to set up the process and organize tasks. For each process step, you can select one or more groups to be involved in the step.
Relative start day: You enter the number of days after the process start. For example, if you enter 0, your process step starts when the process starts; if you enter 2, the process step starts 2 days after the process starts.
Relative end day: The number shown here is calculated based on the step's relative start date and duration. For example, if you enter 4 for the relative start day and 3 for the duration, this field shows 6, which means the process step will end when day 6 of the process ends.

Step settings: You can automate process steps to spare your process step owner from starting and finishing them manually. To do this, you must define conditions for the automatic start and end of the step. You can also decide whether an application job should be triggered automatically at the start or end of the step. Each of these settings can be configured independently for the step start and end.
Automatic start step: You can choose whether the start date of your step's automatic start should depend on the completion of an application job or the completion of the previous step. If you click Custom, you can also combine these conditions.
Automatic end step: You can choose whether the end date of your step's automatic end should depend on the completion of tasks or the completion of an application job. If you click Custom, you can also combine these conditions.
Start job template: If you assign an application job template here, the system automatically triggers an application job based on this template once the process step has started. You can only assign application job templates that you have access to.
End job template: If you assign an application job template here, the system automatically triggers an application job based on this template after the process step has finished. You can only assign application job templates that you have access to.

Although these processes have different names and levels of detail for different companies, most companies represent their processes as follows: ABC/XYZ segmentation, reviewing and correcting historical data, running a statistical forecast, and creating the final demand plan.
###### Manage Process Template
The main way to monitor progress is to track the status of the individual process steps. It provides a view of the process showing whether a process step has been started, is in progress or has been completed.

You can start a process step manually using different applications. You can start a process step from the Manage Processes application or, if you use the Dashboards – Advanced application, from the process chart on your dashboard.

There may be different requirements that must be met before a process step is started or completed. For example, the step of creating the final demand plan can only start after all previous process steps have been completed. In addition, it may be necessary to run certain jobs at the start or end of a process step, such as running the copy operator when the statistical demand planning step ends while creating a demand stage. All of this must be coordinated by the people responsible for the process steps.

The automation feature supports these people in managing process steps and reduces the manual tasks they need to perform. For example, you can configure process steps to start or complete automatically when a certain condition is met. Similarly, you can select application jobs to run automatically after a process step has been started or completed.

What Is SAP?

SAP, Europe's largest software company, was founded in Germany in 1972. Since its founding, the company has been developing software in the field of business applications and is the world leader in this area. The company opened its Türkiye office in 2001, and the software it develops has more than one hundred thousand users in Türkiye from more than twenty industries. At the same time, by bringing all processes and systems together, they help make needs easier to understand and produce quick solutions. In doing so, they do not require many technical staff and they reduce many operational costs to a minimum. After the system goes live, business units start speaking the same language, so harmony within the company increases, the quality of the work produced rises, and all of this is reflected in the organization's profitability. Moreover, using SAP applications contributes positively to companies' market value.

SAP Modules

We can look at SAP ERP applications under two main headings: core module applications and area-based applications.1. SAP ERP Core Module Applications

1.a. Financial Applications

Financial applications contain the structures through which a company can manage its finance and accounting processes. SAP modules such as FI (financial accounting), CO (cost accounting), IM (investment management), FM (funds management), TR (treasury), EC (enterprise controlling) and the RE module (real estate and lease management) are part of the financial applications.

1.b. Logistics Applications

The structures in which a company's operational processes are tracked can be covered under operational applications. SD (sales and distribution), MM (materials management), PP (production planning), WM (warehouse management), PM (plant maintenance), SM (service management), PS (project systems) and QM (quality management) are the modules in SAP where operational processes are tracked.

1.c. Human Resources Applications

These are the applications used to manage all of a company's human resources processes. They have many functions for tracking employees' performance, goals, payroll, personnel records, career development, recruitment processes and training processes. In recent years, SAP's cloud-based human resources solution SuccessFactors has been rapidly increasing its market share both worldwide and in Türkiye, thanks to advantages such as its capabilities, ease of use, mobile compatibility and the global standards in its own library. 2. Area-Based SAP Applications

SAP's Development Over the Years

Looking at SAP's roadmap, we see that in 1974 the focus was mostly on manufacturing. We see that many modules were developed with SAP's R2 version in the 1980s and that the move to R3 took place in the 1990s. Since 2015, we see products running on the HANA infrastructure coming to the fore with S/4HANA technology.

What Is SAP HANA?

Before HANA technology, SAP applications were integrated with databases such as Oracle, DB2 and SQL, and read-write speed could not exceed a certain level. This caused serious slowness, especially in reporting. With the new database it developed under the name HANA (High performance analytic appliance), SAP completely changed the database structure it used and moved to an in-memory structure. Thanks to in-memory technology, RAM began to be used instead of hard disks, and processing speed became far faster than before. SAP also certified HANA servers and announced that it would only support systems installed on certified servers. As a result, many hardware manufacturers such as IBM, HP, Huawei, Lenovo, EMC and Dell brought certified servers developed for the HANA platform to market.

Because SAP applications run on SAP's own database HANA, and the HANA platform runs on certified hardware, an integrated structure has been achieved, just like with the iPhone. Unlike before, we can reach all data through a single structure. As a result, we end up with a platform that runs with high efficiency.

SAP's Product Variety

As of today, SAP has more than 1,800 products. All of these products can be integrated with the HANA platform. In parallel with concepts that have emerged in recent years such as the cloud, big data and mobility, SAP has also started to focus on cloud-based products. In fact, SAP products such as SuccessFactors, Ariba, Hybris, Concur and Fieldglass run only on a cloud basis.

Instant Producer

A recording tool that helps all end users quickly create recordings, training content and training documents. Instant Producer can work integrated with Producer.
Producer

An advanced recording creation tool. It can edit recorded training content and document it in different formats (Word, PDF, HTML, PowerPoint). It supports 40 different languages.
Manager

A component installed on a server. Recordings made with Instant Producer and Producer in different environments, and the content created, can be collected at a common point and made accessible to end users over the web. This way, at the e-learning level, only that department can view the transactions made by different departments in the system.
Navigator

Called the desktop module. It can be used as a desktop application without needing a server connection. Within the application, it helps the end user and suggests field values.

SAP WPB saves the recordings it makes with Producer in 4 different ways;

–    Demo

–    Practice

–    Test

–    Concurrent
Demo

Presents the recorded transaction to the end user step by step as a video.

Practice

Unlike the demo, it tells the end user what needs to be done step by step, has the user perform the transaction, and moves to the next step after the user has done it. If a different action from the one indicated is taken, a notification appears on screen, and with this notification the user can try again or move on to the next step.

Test

In this mode, it asks the user for every action. It gives no hints. If the correct action is taken, it moves to the next step; if a wrong action is taken, a notification appears as in practice mode, and the user can try again or move on to the next step.

Concurrent

In this mode, it performs the steps itself but, unlike the demo, asks the end user to move past the steps or asks for them to be repeated.

Don't Let Your Data Slip Away Unanalyzed!

There is also a lot of data in companies' business processes that slips away without being analyzed. Running analyses on this big data with intelligent algorithms offers very different perspectives for examining and improving even the smallest processes.
We can say that we are in the midst of a revolution far greater than the internet and mobile communication revolutions. Artificial intelligence and big data technologies are developing very fast and are starting to move more and more hand in hand with each passing day. Artificial intelligence, widely used in industries where decision-making is carried out by intelligent machines, now also needs big data management in order to make smarter decisions.
Today, alongside artificial intelligence, big data and its analysis have become promising, game-changing technologies for companies from many different industries. Companies that can incorporate these technologies into their processes can control their business processes more easily and improve them with more accurate decisions, and can also easily produce analyses that are hard to obtain with classic analysis methods and put decision-making mechanisms into operation faster and more effectively.
According to research, about 44 trillion GB of data is created around the world every day. While some of this data is kept on systems, most of it slips away without being analyzed. There is also a lot of data in companies' business processes that slips away without being analyzed. Running analyses on this big data with intelligent algorithms offers very different perspectives for examining and improving even the smallest processes. It is hardly possible to collect and analyze this data with human effort alone. At this point, collecting big data, running instant analyses, and having software run analyses on the data with machine learning algorithms, build its own learning models instead of static instructions and deliver predictions and decisions based on these models gives organizations a great analytical capability. Implementing this kind of project is of great importance for Industry 4.0, which consists of autonomous systems and robotic structures that learn on their own, improve processes and increase production with these gains. The Internet of Things, used to describe objects that talk to one another, is one of the most important figures of this ecosystem. The automation systems and industrial robotic machines used at every point of production each generate dozens of data points every second. The total of the data produced by all machines throughout the production process is, we can say, enormous. These machines, which can talk to one another, produce a tremendous amount of data reporting maintenance needs, production quality, performance values and possible problems. Since it is impossible for a human being to analyze this data to draw conclusions and identify insights within a reasonable time, systems equipped with big data management and artificial intelligence let manufacturers know what to pay attention to and optimize their operations quickly and efficiently.
Speaking of sample implementation projects, when we bring together the right technologies that take on different roles in this kind of project, it is possible to launch projects that can produce very useful results and directly increase efficiency, production capacity and therefore companies' profitability. We can list certain basic steps of such a sample project integrated into the production process as follows:
– Collecting real-time big data from automation systems and robotic machines through sensors

– Defining non-system external factor parameters, determining analysis parameters and designing intelligent machine learning algorithms

– Running real-time and periodic analyses of this data

– Visualizing analysis and decision results in analytical reporting systems

At the point of collecting data from machines, sensors that send certain measurement values need to be positioned. Then a system such as SAP's "SAP HANA Smart Data Integration", which offers intelligent, real-time data integration, can be placed at the center of the project. This way, data arriving instantly from thousands of sensors goes through real-time intelligent transformations, and with the integration of SAP HANA and the "R" programming language, widely used in data analysis, production processes can be improved through findings such as preventive maintenance, detection of impending failures, instant quality tracking of the part being produced, and immediately pulling the part out of the production process and sending it for inspection in case of a possible fault. Likewise, through periodic analyses, recommendations and decisions can be obtained from the system on which parameters can be changed and how to achieve higher-quality and faster production. To visualize the results and recommendations, a solution that offers real-time reporting and reporting cockpit capabilities, such as SAP Analytics Cloud, can be used. For example, a person responsible on the production line can receive an alert on the monitor in front of them or on mobile devices at the moment a robot welds the part being produced, about a possible production error and the likelihood of the weld being low quality, check it immediately and pull the part off the production line.

Building systems in which data turns into information and information into decisions that enable improvement and development always returns profitability to companies many times over the investment made, in the medium and long term. Although big data analysis, machine learning and the Internet of Things have progressed over time in their own lanes as technologies independent of one another, today they stand as the most important figures of a major technological revolution.

The global pandemic, war, the energy crisis, climate change, access to a skilled workforce, supply chain disruptions, geopolitical tensions… The business world is going through very uncertain and turbulent times because of these concepts we have listed in recent years. On one side, production is interrupted by never-ending zero-COVID measures; on the other, global cooperation slows international trade. The effect of high inflation on commodity and raw material prices endangers supplier relationships; congestion at ports delays shipments. While consumers' purchasing power falls, inventories swell, and successive price hikes trigger inflation. While leaders focus on achieving their growth targets, access to a quality workforce is becoming increasingly difficult as employees' expectations and working habits change rapidly in changing market conditions. . On the other hand, digitalization is prompting us to rethink all familiar processes.

What Is Business Intelligence?

Business intelligence, called business intelligence in English and mostly abbreviated as BI, refers to the processes and tools used to analyze business data, turn it into actionable insights and help everyone in an organization make more informed decisions. Next-generation business intelligence systems, also known as decision support systems (DSS), analyze current and historical data and turn the findings into easy-to-understand report, graph and chart formats that can be shared across the company. The data generated by BI provides meaningful references for comparing how any of the business's processes performed yesterday and today.
Business intelligence turns the data obtained from the activities a business carries out into readable, understandable insights from which conclusions can be drawn for planning next strategic steps. Thanks to business intelligence, businesses can carry out their decision-making processes impartially, based entirely on data in the system. Today, in a period when using innovative technology has become a necessity, it has become accessible to businesses of every scale thanks to next-generation business intelligence tools and the possibilities of cloud platforms.
How Does Business Intelligence Give Businesses a Competitive Advantage?

A survey conducted by BI Survey with the participation of 1,823 BI professionals shows that seven out of ten think business intelligence is important for building a data-driven corporate culture. While building a data-driven culture has been among the top five priorities every year the survey has been conducted, its importance is seen to have grown over the years. After all, professionals recognize that developing a data-driven culture is vital to realizing the company's full potential. Adopting next-generation business intelligence solutions and building a data-driven culture puts businesses ahead in competition thanks to the following advantages:

- Make decisions based on facts: BI tools help managers and employees uncover insights related to their roles and areas of responsibility and use them to make decisions based on facts, not guesses.

- Discover new opportunities early: BI tools make it easier to quickly identify new trends and opportunities. Businesses can also discover their strengths and areas for improvement compared to their competitors through the data provided by BI. This gives businesses the chance to get ahead of their rivals.

- Measure performance: Businesses can track their success through the key performance indicators (KPIs) they define. BI tools also make it easier to determine how well business processes and operational achievements align with these performance indicators.

- Discover problems and improve processes: Thanks to next-generation business intelligence, businesses can identify trends and bottlenecks in areas such as users, production, distribution and cost balance early, and improve their processes more easily.

- Increase efficiency: Business intelligence systems increase efficiency by shortening the time needed for processes such as searching for information, analyzing data and creating reports, and also reduce costs.

- Improve customer and employee experience: Business intelligence applications, which provide a fact-based foundation for process optimization, improve the customer and employee experience with the insights they provide.

- Increase revenue and profitability: The revenue and profitability of businesses that optimize business processes, offer better experiences to their customers and employees, and make more accurate strategic decisions increase in direct proportion. After all, thanks to BI tools, businesses can better discover risks and opportunities and plan according to these findings.
